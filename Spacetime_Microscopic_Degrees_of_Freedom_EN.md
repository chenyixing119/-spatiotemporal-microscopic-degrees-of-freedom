# Spacetime Microscopic Degrees of Freedom: From Temperature to Gravity
## A Common Language for General Relativity and Quantum Mechanics

**Position statement**
This is a translation manual, not a physics theory paper. It introduces no new equations, predicts no new phenomena, and challenges no experimentally verified conclusion of general relativity (GR) or quantum mechanics (QM). It retells the worlds described by GR and QM in one language.

## Entry: three facts
1. GR is perfect: it accurately describes gravity, spacetime curvature, black holes and cosmic expansion; all predictions are experimentally confirmed.
2. QM is perfect: it accurately describes microscopic particles, atomic structure and fundamental interactions; all predictions are experimentally confirmed.
3. They are linguistically incompatible near the Planck scale: not because either is wrong, but because two correct languages fail to meet on the same problem.

## Step 1: two states
Diffuse state: like ultra-high-temperature early plasma; quarks, gluons and photons mixed, no fixed position, no boundary, high-energy and active. Note: diffuse state is not "nothing". A photon is a typical diffuse state: it has energy, momentum, frequency and wavelength but no rest mass; it is a physically real quantum entity, only not bound to a fixed position.
Condensed state: like protons, neutrons and atoms; structured, bounded, low-energy and stable.
Very high temperature -> fully diffuse; lower temperature -> gradual condensation (quarks -> hadrons -> nuclei -> atoms). The universe cools continuously from a hot big bang, so diffuse units keep entering condensed states.

## Step 2: temperature to condensation, where the energy goes
Diffuse is high-energy, condensed is low-energy; condensation releases energy as radiation.
- Initial condensation: early quarks and gluons cool and are confined into hadrons, releasing radiation.
- Deepening condensation: fusion (four protons -> helium nucleus). Before fusion the plasma is a low-condensation condensed state; after fusion it is a higher-condensation nuclear bound state, and the potential-energy drop is released as E=mc².
Second law sets direction (high -> low energy spontaneously); energy conservation ensures: released radiation + condensed potential = initial diffuse total energy.

## Step 3: condensation and symmetry breaking
Diffuse state has high symmetry; condensation must "choose" a direction/phase, so symmetry breaks. Condensation = symmetry breaking. Electromagnetism U(1), weak SU(2), strong SU(3) correspond to different breaking patterns. Whether this implies one underlying process is not asserted here; we only note that different forces formally share symmetry-breaking features.

## Step 4: stability of condensed states
Glueball: self-bound gluons; mass entirely from gluon interaction energy. Lattice QCD predicts lifetime ~10⁻²³ s; glueballs are not yet experimentally confirmed, so all such numbers are theoretical predictions/candidate signals, not observed values.
Proton: experimental lower-limit lifetime >10³⁴ yr; no proton decay observed.
Stability needs two conditions:
1) low enough energy (thermodynamics);
2) stable anchor (topology-like, like a tied knot unchanged under continuous deformation). Proton has three-quark color-singlet anchor; glueball has no quark-core anchor and is destroyed by its own fluctuations.

## Step 5: from condensation to mass
Two layers:
First, rest mass = total amount of condensed units. Diffuse units have no fixed position/trajectory, hence no inertial mass in that sense; once condensed they are fixed, moving them needs force, i.e. inertia; tighter condensation -> larger rest mass.
Second, gravitational source = stress-energy tensor (GR). Condensed states contribute rest mass and internal energy; diffuse states (e.g. photons) contribute momentum and radiation energy. Both can source gravity, but condensed states are the main static source; diffuse states matter strongly only at very high energy density (e.g. early universe).
E=mc² means: rest mass is a measure of condensed-state energy density.

## Step 6: from condensation to gravity
Picture: diffuse state as boiling energy ocean; condensed network as a deep well ("low energy" means low interaction potential and stable state, not low temperature). A diffuse unit approaching acquires more connections to anchors -> level drops; nearer -> more connections -> lower level -> naturally slides downhill; this tendency is gravity.
In this language:
1) condensed network consists of many condensed units and stable anchors;
2) diffuse unit approaching -> more connections -> lower level;
3) drop grows with proximity -> gravitational potential difference;
4) diffuse unit moves along the steepest descent of the energy gradient -> toward the network.
Attraction reason: if energy drops on approach, it attracts; if it rises (e.g. same-sign charges), it repels.
Points:
- diffuse -> condensed network: level drops -> attracted;
- diffuse-diffuse attraction exists but is very weak under ordinary density; only at very high energy density (early universe) does it become dominant — consistent with Step 5 layer two;
- two condensed networks approaching: gradients superimpose -> stronger gravity; their overall motion is translated by GR geodesics, e.g. planet orbiting the Sun.
One line: gravity is not the network "pulling" the diffuse state, but the diffuse state naturally sliding toward the network while seeking lower energy.

## Step 7: translate GR
GR "mass curves spacetime, bodies follow geodesics" -> mass = total condensation (rest mass + internal energy); spacetime curvature = geometric description of the energy gradient that a condensed network imposes on external diffuse states; geodesic = path of fastest descent along that gradient. Same phenomenon, two languages.

## Step 8: translate QM
Uncertainty: diffuse state is an infinite probability wave, indefinite position and definite momentum; condensed state is confined, more definite position and broader momentum; partially condensed lies between. Essence: diffuse units are probabilistic by nature; only after condensation do they show definite position/momentum. A probability wave describes occurrence likelihood, not classical wave vibration.
Wavefunction = probability distribution of a diffuse unit; modulus squared gives position probability. After condensation the position is definite -> wavefunction "collapses".
Measurement collapse = a diffuse unit forced into a definite state by a dense condensed network; microscopically it involves environment entanglement (decoherence), macroscopically an irreversible definite result.

## Step 9: GR-QM conflict, two ends of a continuum
Left end diffuse -> QM-like (high energy, high symmetry, probabilistic); right end condensed -> GR-like (low energy, low symmetry, deterministic). Each is perfect at its extreme; near Planck scale the languages fail to match.
Note: both theories actually span the whole spectrum. "Left QM, right GR" is only an intuitive simplification — QM works on condensed matter (solid-state physics), GR works on diffuse radiation (cosmological radiation era). The "middle" refers to the region where the mathematical structures of the two languages diverge near the Planck scale, not the midpoint of this material-state spectrum. This framework offers no mathematical solution there.

## Step 10: boundaries
We do: unified translation of GR/QM; explain what mass is, why gravity attracts, and the essence of uncertainty; show that the conflict comes from the two ends of one continuum.
We do not: introduce new equations, predict new phenomena, or overturn any GR/QM experimental conclusion.
Notes: 1/r² comes from GR weak-field limit and spacetime dimensionality; this framework only explains "why attract", not quantitative form. The equivalence principle is paralleled as "diffuse state sliding freely along a gradient with no external force", not claimed as a mathematical reduction.
Open problem: this framework gives only conceptual language, no mathematical form. Interested readers may try to formulate the "energy gradient of a condensed network on diffuse states" mathematically, or relate it to GR geodesics and QFT path integrals. The framework prescribes no answer; it only offers a starting point.

## Step 11: close
GR remains perfect, QM remains perfect; they are not incompatible but use different languages for the two ends of one continuum. This framework replaces no theory; it only translates.

## Appendix 1: glueballs
Gluons have no rest mass; glueball mass comes entirely from gluon interaction energy. Lattice QCD: mass ~1.5–2.0 GeV (by quantum numbers), lifetime ~10⁻²³ s (unconfirmed experimentally; theoretical/candidate only). Proton 0.938 GeV, >10³⁴ yr no observed decay. Glueball lacks quark anchor -> fluctuates apart; proton has three-quark color-singlet anchor -> stable.

## Appendix 2: fusion
4p → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2νₑ + 26.73 MeV. Four protons 4×1.007825 u = 4.03130 u; helium nucleus 4.002603 u; deficit 0.028697 u × 931.494 = 26.73 MeV. Framework reading: pre-fusion low-condensation plasma, post-fusion higher-condensation nuclear bound state, potential drop emitted as radiation, mass deficit.

## Appendix 3: comparison with unification programs
String theory: one-dimensional strings in extra dimensions for unified forces; this framework uses no extra dimensions and no new modeling. Loop quantum gravity: spin networks quantize spacetime; our "condensed network" is only conceptually similar, not formalized. Holography: 3D physics dual to 2D boundary; not used here. This framework: condense/diffuse binary language translating GR/QM, no new predictions, conceptual only; its merit is simplicity, not mathematics.
